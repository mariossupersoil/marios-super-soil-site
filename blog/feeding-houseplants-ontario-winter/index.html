    Feeding houseplants in an Ontario winter

    Published 2026-11-17

    If your houseplants look a little sleepy since the clocks changed, that is normal. Ontario days are short from November to February, and most indoor plants respond by growing slowly or not at all.

    Why winter changes feeding

    Plants use nutrients to build new leaves and roots. With less daylight and cooler windowsills, many houseplants make little new growth, so they use less of what is in the pot. Feeding at the same rate as summer can leave unused nutrients sitting in the soil.

    A simple winter routine

    • Check for new growth first. New leaves or roots mean the plant is still active and can be fed lightly. No growth usually means pause.
    • Go lighter, not heavier. Many growers cut back a lot, or skip feeding until late winter. Follow the directions on whatever you use.
    • Water less often. Let the top of the soil dry to the depth your plant prefers before watering. Cold, wet soil is a common winter problem.
    • Move plants off cold glass. Windowsills in an Ontario January can be much colder than the room.
    • Keep plants away from heating vents. Dry, hot air stresses leaves.
    • Do not feed a struggling plant. If a plant is stressed, wilting or pest-affected, fix the cause first.

    Planning ahead

    As days lengthen in late February and March, watch for new growth and begin feeding again at a normal rate.
