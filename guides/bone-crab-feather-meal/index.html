Bone, crab, feather, kelp and alfalfa meal compared

Short answer: Feather meal (11-0-0) is for nitrogen, bone meal (4-10-0) is for phosphorus, crab meal (4-3-0) adds nitrogen with calcium, kelp meal (1-0-4) adds potassium and ocean minerals, and alfalfa meal (3-0.5-3) is a gentle all-round boost.

Feather meal: strong, slow nitrogen

Feather Meal is 11-0-0, our richest nitrogen source, for lush leaves. Use 1 to 2 tablespoons per gallon of soil or coco and avoid overapplying.

Bone meal: phosphorus for roots, blooms and fruit

Bone Meal is 4-10-0. Use 1 to 2 tablespoons per gallon of soil, mix in and water lightly. Its nutrients release over about 4 months.

Crab meal: nitrogen plus calcium

Crab Meal is 4-3-0 and brings calcium from the shell. Use 1 to 2 teaspoons per gallon of soil, applied in spring and again every 2 to 3 months.

Kelp meal: potassium and ocean minerals

Kelp Meal is 1-0-4 and supplies trace minerals from the sea. Mix 1 to 2 tablespoons per gallon of soil and apply monthly in the growing season.

Alfalfa meal: a gentle growth boost

Alfalfa Meal is 3-0.5-3. Mix 2 tablespoons per gallon of soil and repeat in spring as needed.

Which one should I start with?

If you want one bag for general use, a balanced blend like Level Up 3-3-3 or Flower Power 2-6-4 combines several of these. If you want to target a gap, pick the single meal that matches it. The calculator can help you decide.

Questions

What is the difference between bone meal and crab meal?

Bone meal (4-10-0) is mostly a phosphorus source. Crab meal (4-3-0) gives nitrogen with calcium from crab shell.

Which meal has the most nitrogen?

Feather meal, at 11-0-0.

How long does bone meal last in soil?

Our bone meal releases its nutrients over about 4 months.

Can I combine different meals?

Yes. Many gardeners combine a nitrogen, a phosphorus and a potassium source. Follow each bag's dose and avoid overapplying.
