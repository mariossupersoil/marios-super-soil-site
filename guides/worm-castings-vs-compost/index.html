Worm castings vs compost

Short answer: Compost adds bulk organic matter that improves soil structure, while worm castings are a concentrated, biologically active amendment used in smaller amounts. Many gardeners use both.

At a glance

AmendmentNPKBest known for
Worm Castings1.5 - 2.5 - 1.3Live biology for any soil
Compost Blend0.7 - 0.3 - 0.6Organic matter that builds humus

Worm castings

Worm Castings are the finished waste of earthworms, often called vermicast. They add live biology and small amounts of iron, calcium, magnesium, sulfur and other minerals. Mix them into potting soil, or use 1 to 2 cups around established plants and water thoroughly.

Compost

Compost Blend is a balanced mix of decomposed organic materials that improves the physical, chemical and biological properties of soil. Mix 1 part compost with 4 parts soil, or spread it on beds and lawns. It can also be steeped as compost tea by aerating it in water for 24 to 48 hours.

Which should I use?

  • Use compost in bulk to improve a bed's structure and water holding.
  • Use worm castings for a concentrated boost of living material in pots, seed starting mixes and around plants.
  • Use both with a nutrient amendment such as a meal for a complete plan.

Questions

Are worm castings the same as compost?

No. Worm castings are earthworm waste. Compost is decomposed organic matter. Both improve soil, but castings are more concentrated.

How much worm castings should I use?

Mix into potting soil, or use 1 to 2 cups around established plants and water thoroughly.

Can I use worm castings and compost together?

Yes. Use compost to build the bed and castings as a concentrated boost.

Does compost have an NPK number?

Our compost blend is about 0.7-0.3-0.6. Its main value is organic matter and soil life, not NPK.
