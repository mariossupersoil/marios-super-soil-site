    Organic amendments for pots and containers

    Short answer: Mix dry amendments into your potting soil before planting, using the per-gallon dose on the bag, usually 1 to 2 tablespoons per gallon, and add more only as each bag directs.

    Why dry amendments suit containers

    Pots hold a limited amount of soil, so the dose is given per gallon of soil. Dry amendments are mixed in once and break down slowly, which means less frequent feeding and a gentler result.

    Per-gallon doses

    AmendmentDose per gallon of soil
    Flower Power1 tbsp (mature plants 2 tbsp, seedlings 1/2 tbsp)
    Bone Meal1 to 2 tbsp
    Feather Meal1 to 2 tbsp
    Kelp Meal1 to 2 tbsp, monthly in the growing season
    Alfalfa Meal2 tbsp
    Crab Meal1 to 2 tsp
    Oyster Shell Flour1 to 2 tbsp (new pots)
    Gypsum1 to 2 tbsp
    Dolomite Lime1 to 2 tbsp
    Soft Rock Phosphate2 tbsp
    Basalt Rock Dust1 tbsp

    Doses come from the bags. Start at the low end and do not stack several amendments without checking the calculator.

    A simple potting mix recipe

    Mix Worm Castings into your potting soil, then add one nutrient amendment that matches your plant: a nitrogen meal for leafy plants, or bone meal or Flower Power for flowering and fruiting plants. Water it in well.

    Use the soil amendment calculator to get amounts for your pot size.

    Questions

    How much amendment do I put in a pot?

    Usually 1 to 2 tablespoons per gallon of soil. Check the bag for each product.

    How often should I feed potted plants?

    Mix once at planting, and top up as each bag directs. Kelp meal is applied monthly in the growing season.

    Can I use these indoors?

    Yes. They are used indoors and outdoors. Follow the directions on each bag.

    Can I plant in the empty bag?

    Yes. The pouches are recyclable and reusable.
