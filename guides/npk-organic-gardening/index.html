    NPK explained for organic gardeners

    Short answer: NPK stands for nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K), the three main nutrients plants use. The three numbers on a bag, such as 4-10-0 for bone meal, are the percentages of each by weight.

    What do the three numbers mean?

    The first number is nitrogen, the second is phosphorus and the third is potassium. Bone meal at 4-10-0 contains 4% nitrogen, 10% phosphorus and 0% potassium.

    • Nitrogen (N) drives leaf and stem growth and a deep green colour.
    • Phosphorus (P) supports roots, flowers and fruit.
    • Potassium (K) supports overall plant strength and water balance.

    Why organic NPK numbers look lower

    Organic amendments release nutrients as soil life breaks them down, so the numbers are lower than in fast-dissolving products. They also carry calcium, magnesium, trace minerals and organic matter that the numbers do not show. Mineral amendments such as gypsum, dolomite lime, oyster shell flour and basalt rock dust show little or no NPK because their job is calcium, magnesium, sulfur and trace minerals.

    Matching the amendment to the job

    For leafy growth, use a nitrogen source like Feather Meal or Alfalfa Meal. For roots and blooms, choose Bone Meal or Soft Rock Phosphate. For overall strength, add Kelp Meal. Round it out with Worm Castings to feed soil life. The free calculator works out amounts for your bed or pot.

    Questions

    What does NPK mean?

    Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. The numbers on a bag are the percentage of each by weight.

    Which organic amendment is highest in nitrogen?

    Blood meal at 12-0-0 is the highest nitrogen amendment we make, followed by feather meal at 11-0-0.

    Which organic amendment is highest in phosphorus?

    Bone meal at 4-10-0 is the highest phosphorus amendment we make.

    Why do some amendments show 0-0-0?

    Mineral amendments such as gypsum, dolomite lime, oyster shell flour and basalt rock dust are used for calcium, magnesium, sulfur and trace minerals, not for NPK.
